1. The company

The app + B2B donor pipeline + educator + enterprise wellness all need different lifecycle journeys; current state likely heroic-spreadsheet-driven.

Five anchors I can pull from for any behavioral prompt. Each one tagged with the dimensions it covers.

LeadershipCross-functional

Squad Lead promotion at Merck (2022)

SituationThree-system program (CDDR, CDI, SLS) lost the previous PM. Cross-functional product squad (8 people, Biostatistics + Medical Writing + Research Decision Sciences) was drifting.
TaskGet the squad re-aligned, ship year objectives on schedule, keep zero audit findings.
ActionFirst two weeks I did 1:1s with every squad member to find the actual blockers. Restructured sprint cadence (backlog grooming Mondays, sprint planning Tuesdays, retros every other Friday). Reduced active stories per sprint by 30%, forced sequencing decisions instead of parallelism. Brought in mentorship as a deliberate practice — paired senior analysts with newer ones.
ResultYear objectives shipped on schedule. Zero audit findings across all three systems. Got the OUTPERFORMED rating for two years running. The note from Jenny Mao (Cornell Tech intern, summer 2025) is the one I think about more than the rating.

China NDA translation cycle (2024-2025)

SituationTranslated Clinical Study Reports and supporting NDA documents took 6-7 months to deliver to China's NMPA. The bottleneck was killing regulatory submission timelines, which kept medicines from patients longer.
TaskCut cycle time meaningfully without compromising regulatory accuracy.
ActionDirect user research with stakeholders in Tokyo, Shanghai, Berlin, Brussels, and Boston. Designed the A&R Submission Translation platform around what they actually needed (parallel translation streams, source-of-truth versioning, regulator-facing format compliance). FailureRecovery

KiddieSketch Meta Ads — broken pixel diagnostic

SituationKiddieSketch Meta Ads burning ~$18/day for 30 days with zero attributed purchases. ROAS effectively zero.
TaskDiagnose root cause and recover the spend trajectory.
ActionPulled the ad-set state via Meta Graph API instead of trusting the UI. Found two root causes: all four ad sets optimizing for LINK_CLICKS instead of PURCHASE, AND firing the legacy OzzyMart pixel (292534870409133) instead of the live KB-KS-Worldwide pixel (1284026633251044). Wrote up the fix as a runbook and prioritized the lowest-spend ad set first to limit Learning-phase risk.
ResultFix in progress; CTR stayed strong (4.55%) and CPC stayed low ($0.14) throughout, which means the creative was working — only the attribution was broken. Lesson: trust the API, not the UI, when numbers don't match expectations.
InnovationRepeatability

KiddieWordle 17-language architecture

SituationWanted to ship a free word game for kids that compounded — every language unlocks a new audience, but I couldn't afford to write a custom version per language.
TaskDesign a content-localization system where the marginal cost of adding language N+1 is bounded.
ActionBuilt the engine once with locale switching baked into URL structure (/[lang]/). Hreflang + structured data per locale. Five-step process per new language: source kid-safe word list from public-domain corpus, generate locale routing, write one explainer post, submit sitemap, add GA4 dimension. Total time per new language: under 4 hours.
Result17 languages live with AdSense instrumented. Marketing rollout scheduled for June. The architecture is the win — adding languages 18, 19, 20 doesn't compound the work.

Craft / technical

"Walk me through a lifecycle program you've built end-to-end."

KiddieSketch on Klaviyo. Four segments: new-no-purchase, first-time customers, repeat customers (2+ orders), and gift-givers identified by shipping address differing from billing address. Four flows: welcome series (4 emails, 7 days, 20% offer immediately), abandoned cart (3 emails over 48 hours, no incentive on email 1 — just reassurance), post-purchase (5 emails including a referral ask at day 14), win-back (90-day trigger). Triggers via Shopify Flow + Make.com for cross-system glue. A/B tested subject lines, send times, and the welcome lead magnet. The gift-giver segmentation changed the program's voice in a way that mattered — a grandmother buying a print for her grandson reads differently from a parent buying for their own child.

"How would you scale lifecycle at Humin?"

Automate first, delegate second, hire third. Most of the growth pain in mission-driven orgs is smart people doing repetitive lifecycle work by hand because the systems were never built to scale with them. I'd start with an audit: which journeys exist, which fire automatically, which require someone to push a button, which are governed by a spreadsheet. Then I'd build the high-leverage automation first (behaviorally triggered journeys keyed off app-usage signals, segmentation that updates without a quarterly rebuild, reporting the team can trust). Then I'd document for delegation. Then if scale still demands it, hire — but hire after the systems are built, so the new person inherits leverage instead of debt. I'm doing exactly this in my own operation right now — hiring contract help in India to absorb execution-level work across my brands.

"What's your relationship with the Healthy Minds Program app?"

User since 2024. I picked it up alongside the MHFA cert. It's in my regular rotation as part of my daily Vedic practice — the science-based framing matches how my brain works. Honest opinion as a user: the content is the strongest part; the lifecycle around the content is where there's real room to grow. The app has the credibility to do more aggressive cross-property loops with other Healthy Minds offerings if it wanted to.

Mission / curveballs

"What's a non-obvious thing you'd change in our marketing in your first 90 days?"

Segment the audience by stage of practice instead of (or in addition to) by demographic. Someone who's day 3 of the app needs a fundamentally different lifecycle than someone who's day 90 or day 365. If you're segmenting by life-stage or persona alone, you're missing the most predictive signal you have. Bonus: that segmentation maps cleanly to the donor pipeline too — long-term practitioners are the ones who self-identify as ready to fund the org.

5. 30-60-90 day plan

My proposed plan if hired. Use this verbatim in interviews; iterate after onboarding.

Days 1-30 · Audit + listen

  • 1:1s with the VP of Marketing, every team peer, and 5 cross-functional partners (product, content, science, ops, fundraising)
  • Audit current lifecycle: every active flow, what triggers it, what segments it uses, what metrics it reports
  • Audit the Mailchimp / HubSpot stack — what's actually in use vs. what's history
  • Read the last 12 months of app retention + cohort data
  • Use the Healthy Minds Program app daily as a user, document friction
  • Map the donor + enterprise + educator + consumer journeys and where they intersect
  • Don't change anything yet

Days 31-60 · Propose + validate

  • Present audit findings to VP Marketing with three prioritized lifecycle bets
  • Validate the top bet with a 2-week test (likely a behavioral journey keyed off app-usage signal)
  • Stand up a single source of truth for lifecycle metrics — a Looker Studio dashboard that VP Marketing can show the board without preparing it manually
  • Document segmentation framework v1 (stage of practice + audience type)
  • Set up the Slack rituals for lifecycle reporting (weekly digest)
  • Begin the consolidation conversation: which tools simplify, which stay

Days 61-90 · Ship + measure

  • Ship the first automated behavioral journey end-to-end
  • Roll out the segmentation framework across the active flows
  • Demonstrate measurable lift on the top metric the VP committed to (likely activation or D30 retention)
  • Document the playbook so it's portable
  • Define the 6-month roadmap with the team
  • Stand up the cross-property growth loop (app → newsletter → enterprise pipeline) if the data supports it

6. Questions for them

Asking sharp questions is half the signal of being a good hire. Pick 2-3 per round, never 7.

For recruiter

  • What does "success at six months" look like in the VP's head, in concrete metrics?
  • Is this a backfill or a new role? If backfill, what happened?
  • Where's the team currently strongest, and where is the biggest gap?
  • How does headcount this year compare to last year?

For hiring manager (VP of Marketing)

  • What's the biggest current pain in the lifecycle program — list growth, activation, retention, donor cultivation, or something else?
  • What tools are mid-flight that you'd want this hire to push forward or sunset?
  • How do you think about the relationship between consumer growth and enterprise / donor pipelines? Do they share a CRM, or are they separate worlds?
  • What's the relationship between marketing and the science / research arm of Healthy Minds — partnership, customer, or content source?
  • What's the team's rhythm — async, sync, ritual? What does a typical week look like for this role?

For peer interviewers

  • What's the team's biggest constraint right now — headcount, tools, leadership air-cover, or something else?
  • What's the unwritten rule of this team that you'd want a new hire to know on day one?
  • Who's the easiest person to collaborate with here, and who's the hardest? (Honest answers signal a healthy team.)

For executive interviewers

  • What's the three-year mission roadmap and where does growth marketing show up in it?
  • What's the org's stance on AI in the wellness space — is that something you'd want this role to push forward, or hold the line on?
  • If this hire goes well, what does Humin look like in two years that it doesn't look like today?
