2. Lifecycle or CRM system you've built

For KiddieSketch (Shopify store with 4 active SKUs averaging $30-60 AOV), I designed and operate the full Klaviyo lifecycle program. The architecture has been running across iterations for the past several months; the program is currently mid-rebuild as we refresh the Klaviyo OAuth connection. Segmentation. I split subscribers into four primary segments. (1) New-no-purchase, subscribers who joined the list but have not yet ordered. (2) First-time customers. (3) Repeat customers with two or more orders. (4) Gift-givers, identified by shipping address differing from billing address. The gift-giver segment changes the program's voice in a real way. A grandmother buying a print for her grandson responds to different copy than a parent buying for their own child. Automation flows designed, authored, and operating across iterations: • Welcome series. Email 1 sends immediately on signup with a 20% off welcome offer (no discount code needed; auto-applies at checkout). Subsequent emails build trust by surfacing real customer outcomes rather than product catalogs. • Abandoned cart, three emails over 48 hours. Email 1 at T+1 hour with no incentive, just reassurance that the sketch is saved ("Did your art make it through?" subject line, parent-to-parent tone). Email 2 at T+24 hours with a light, time-boxed incentive. Email 3 at T+48 hours. The flow suppresses if Placed Order has fired in the last 4 days, so it never steps on other flows. • Post-purchase, production-update sequence plus a referral ask at 14 days post-delivery. • Win-back at 90 days post-purchase, on a "your child has probably brought home more drawings" theme. Tools. Klaviyo as the email engine. Shopify Flow for behavioral triggers. Make.com for cross-system glue, including a Slack notification when a high-LTV customer triggers a flow. Google Sheets and Looker Studio for reporting that lives outside Klaviyo's native dashboards. Testing. A/B tested subject lines on the welcome series with the gift-giver-targeted variant outperforming the default. Tested send-time delays on the abandoned-cart trigger; a four-hour delay outperformed a one-hour delay on click-through. Iterated the welcome lead magnet four times before landing on the current 20% offer. Results. I will be direct about what I can quantify with current data. Open rates across the program track in the 30-45% range; click rates in the 3-5% range — the kind of small-business-list performance that comes from segmentation discipline rather than from sheer volume. The full quantitative picture is mid-rebuild as the Klaviyo OAuth refresh completes. What I can attest to without hedging is that the flow architecture is mature, the segmentation discipline is real, and the program has been iterated through multiple cycles.

3. Repeatable system for growth and conversion

For KiddieWordle (a free Wordle variant for kids, engineered in seventeen languages with AdSense monetization wired up, launching marketing in the June rollout wave), I designed a repeatable system around localized content as a top-of-funnel growth engine. The thesis: every new language unlocks an organic audience competitors are not serving. Mandarin Wordle for kids. Gujarati Wordle for kids. Tamil Wordle for kids. The search demand exists. The supply for children specifically does not. Content and workflow. The engine was built once, with locale switching baked into the URL structure (kiddiewordle.com/[lang]/) and hreflang plus structured data per locale. Adding each new language follows a repeatable five-step process: source a kid-safe word list from a public-domain corpus for that language, generate the locale-specific routing, write a single language-specific explainer post, submit the new sitemap path to Google Search Console, and add the locale to my GA4 dimension. Total time to add a new language is under four hours. Adding language eighteen, nineteen, and twenty does not compound the workload. Tracking architecture. Per-locale traffic dimensions in GA4. Google Search Console for indexation and click data per language. AdSense reports for revenue per locale. Looker Studio dashboard pulling all three together so I can see, once organic traffic begins arriving post-launch, which languages pay for themselves and which need more content investment. Iteration loop, designed in. The system is wired so that Search Console surfaces which "wordle in [language] for kids" queries land highest-intent per locale. I will re-point homepage H1s in those locales to match the query language. The workflow for this iteration is documented as part of the launch playbook, not improvised after the fact. Conversion definition. KiddieWordle's conversion is not a purchase, since the game is free. The conversions I optimize for are session-to-AdSense-revenue plus newsletter sign-ups from parents seeking kid-safe games. The newsletter sign-ups feed into the KiddieSketch lifecycle program as a cross-property growth loop. That loop is the real point. KiddieWordle is small revenue on its own and meaningful when it feeds users into the rest of the brand portfolio. Where it stands today, honestly. The engine is live at kiddiewordle.com across seventeen languages with AdSense instrumented. Marketing rollout — organic content amplification, parent-community seeding, paid acquisition tests on the top three locales — is scheduled for the June launch wave alongside KiddieGo. What I am presenting in this answer is the system architecture and the repeatability discipline. The traffic and revenue claims will follow the rollout. Built to delegate. The four-hour-per-language workflow is deliberately structured to be executable by contract help, not just by me. I am currently building a small team in India to take ownership of that execution layer for the June rollout. The "repeatable" in "repeatable system" is the operational word — repeatable by someone who is not me. That is the only kind of growth system that actually scales. Why this transfers to Humin. The same principle applies to wellbeing content. Different audience segments — parents, educators, enterprise wellness buyers, individual users at different stages of practice — each have search and content needs that mostly are not being met by mainstream meditation content. A repeatable content-localization system, where localization means audience-segment rather than language, is the same engine. I would build it for Humin the same way I built it for KiddieWordle, just with the audience taxonomy mapped onto your user research rather than onto language codes.
