Some things I'm proud of

Short stories from the work that meant the most. Each one is mine to tell.

Merck · 2023–25

10,000 hours back to the people doing the work

I led the Scale IT for Clinical Portfolio program across four systems at Merck. The win, in plain numbers: about 10,000 hours of manual effort, every year, gone — work people used to grind through, now automated. We delivered ahead of schedule, and the impact is projected to double as adoption spreads. The leadership recognition was nice; the real reward was knowing biostatisticians I'd never meet got their evenings back.

Merck · 2022

6-7 months down to a month — translated CSR for China NMPA

Regulatory submissions to China's NMPA used to take 6-7 months of translating the Clinical Study Report and supporting documents before they could even be filed. I designed and shipped the A&R Submission Translation platform that cut that cycle to about a month. The research wasn't done from a desk — I interviewed users in Tokyo, Shanghai, Berlin, Brussels, and Boston, and the platform was built around what they actually needed. Faster regulatory submissions mean faster access to medicines. That math is hard to argue with.

Merck · 2022

Promoted to Squad Lead, kept showing up for the team

The 2022 promotion to Squad Lead wasn't the part I'm proudest of. The part I'm proudest of is the reverse-mentoring relationship I built with a senior director outside my reporting chain — he was generous enough to write back: "exceptional in every area we explored. I do not have any further development feedback." Leadership is two-way; if I'm coaching anyone, I'm being coached too.

Merck · summers 2023–25

Mentoring Jenny through her summer at Merck

One of the most rewarding parts of the Merck role was mentoring Jenny Mao, a Cornell Tech master's student who spent the summer of 2025 on my team. Weekly 1:1s, panel-style mock interviews, resume rewrites, the "I don't know how to answer this" questions in real time. Her parting note: "I truly don't think I would have completed my internship so successfully without your mentorship." I'll take a paragraph like that over an annual review any day. That is the line I think about.

Independent · 2024–now

Built a free children's word game in 17 languages

KiddieWordle is a Wordle variant for younger players. No accounts, no ads in the play area, no monetization. The reason I'm proud of it: it's in seventeen languages, which means a five-year-old in Kerala can play in Malayalam and a five-year-old in Vermont can play in English, and the experience is the same. Universal access to small, useful things — that's a principle I try to live by.

Independent · 2024–now

Turned my son's drawings into a real business

KiddieSketch came out of a simple question: what if the drawings my son brings home from preschool didn't just live on the fridge? It's a Shopify store that turns kids' art into a real keepsake gift — a framed print, a mug, a hoodie, a lightboard, a photo album. Built end-to-end: the upload flow, the AI sketch enhancer, the spec validator, the multi-fulfillment routing. Every order is someone's kid's drawing landing on someone's grandmother's wall.
